Amply v1.8.3
Release date: unreleased

Amply is free, with Amply Pro
- Amply is free: up to 200 songs from one folder, every format, playback with sleep timer presets,
  lyrics for 10 songs a day, artwork, artist photos, 5 playlists, twelve smart mixes refreshed
  weekly, a week of listening stats, backups and M3U export. No ads.
- Try everything in Pro free for 7 days, once. No card needed; it simply ends.
- Already using Amply? Your whole library keeps playing, and playlists and folders you already have
  stay; the limits only stop new ones.
- Songs past the limit stay in your library with a lock, and Amply tells you how many are waiting.
- Amply Pro is a one-time purchase in the Microsoft Store (no subscription). It plays your whole
  library from every folder (new music shows up on its own), unlimited playlists, lyrics for every
  song with lyrics tools, artist stories, audiophile playback (gapless, volume levelling and your
  choice of output device), any sleep timer length and 0.5x-2x speed, refreshes mixes whenever you
  like, and adds accent colours and a true black theme, smart playlists that fill themselves from rules, Amply Encore, the mini
  player, crossfade, the full equalizer (your own curve plus the Warm and Club presets), genre and
  mood mixes, mix tuning with Last.fm and MusicBrainz suggestions,
  30-day and all-time stats, the tag editor, the duplicate finder, Game Mode and the Aurora and
  Mono visualizer styles.
- The price is shown in your own currency. "Restore purchase" in the Pro dialog picks up a
  purchase made on another PC with the same Microsoft account.
- Your plan (Free or Pro) is shown next to the Amply logo and at the top of Settings.
- If you were already using a feature that is now in Pro, Amply says so once. Your settings are
  kept and come back as soon as you upgrade.
- Listening history keeps recording on the free plan, so upgrading shows all of it.
- Can't afford Pro? After the trial, the Pro dialog lets you ask the developer for a free code.

New in Pro
- Accent colours: rose, violet, blue, teal, green, gold and graphite join amber, and a true black
  dark mode suits OLED screens.
- Smart playlists: build a playlist from rules ("Rock, played at least 5 times, added in the last
  year") and it keeps itself up to date as you listen.
- Amply Encore: your year in music, played back as a deck of colourful cards: minutes listened, your
  sound, your number one artist and song, your top five, your listening personality, your longest
  streak and loudest month. Save a share card, or play a mix of the songs that made your year.

Report a problem
- Settings > App Version > Report a problem sends a bug report or an idea straight to the developer.
  You see exactly what is sent, technical details are optional, and your Windows user name is removed.

Visualizer
- Ember, Aurora and Mono now look different from one another: thin bars with falling peaks, flowing
  colour waves, and a dot-matrix meter. The strip runs edge to edge and tapers at both ends.

Playback
- Crossfade now really overlaps songs at the end of a track; before, the next song faded in after
  the last one had stopped.
- Shuffle plays every song in the queue once before repeating, and the queue panel shows the real
  order. Turning shuffle off goes back to the queue's own order.
- Previous walks back through what you played instead of bouncing between two songs.
- A song deleted or moved while it was queued is skipped instead of stopping playback.
- Turning shuffle on while repeating one song no longer keeps looping that song.
- Removing the playing song from the queue no longer skips the next one.
- Pausing during a crossfade no longer leaves the next song quiet.
- The sleep timer is no longer undone when another app stops playing audio.
- Your queue, Up Next, shuffle, repeat and volume are remembered between launches.
- Closing Amply saves your place and settings before the window goes.

Look and feel
- Softer depth throughout: shadows and highlights are lighter and shorter, so surfaces read as
  gently raised instead of embossed. Switches keep a thin outline so an "off" switch is easy to see.
- Missing artwork shows a quiet music-note icon instead of the words "No image" or "Amply".
- The taskbar and Start icon is the full Amply logo again, not a small logo on a coloured square.
- Song lists have one "Add to playlist" button per row instead of a drop-down in every row, which
  gives titles, albums and genres room to breathe.
- The Home page is called Home, like its sidebar entry.
- Settings lists the keyboard shortcuts.
- Playback speed goes from 0.5x to 2x.

Library
- Smaller libraries get mixes that make sense: no two mixes of the same songs, no mixes of one
  or two songs, Favorites holds every favourite, and more mixes appear as the library grows.
- Clear Cache only clears caches. It used to also erase playlists, favourites, play counts,
  settings and library folders.
- Backups and restores include this session's plays and favourites, and restored playlists follow
  their songs to a new drive letter.
- Files whose names differ only in spaces, punctuation or non-Latin letters, in different folders
  or opened from outside the library, are no longer mixed up with each other.
- Titles read from file names keep words like "Birthday", "Video" or "Clean" that belong to them,
  and titles in any script no longer risk failing the scan.
- The mini player no longer keeps the last cover for a song without one.
- Artist photos load again: Wikipedia moved most of them to a new image server that version 1.7.0
  blocked. Amply now accepts every Wikimedia image server, and if a photo still fails, artist cards
  fall back to the album cover instead of an initial.
